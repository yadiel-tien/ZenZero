0　What this environment is

The Chinese Chess (Xiangqi) environment is the physics layer of ZenZero's AlphaZero loop: MCTS asks it for only three things — get_valid_actions (which moves exist), virtual_step (what results after a move) and check_winner / game_over (whether the game is decided after a move); training sampling additionally asks for convert_to_network (turning the board into a snapshot). It is at the same time the single source of truth for the rules of play: human vs AI, self-play, the arena, Piskvork and the web backend all run on one and the same adjudication.

Feature epoch 2 (2026-10-27), two upgrades: ① the rules move from the capture-the-king convention to the official legal-move formulation (leaving one's own king en prise is illegal; checkmate and stalemate are scored as losses under one rule); ② the board representation is re-laid out from 20 planes (pieces + difference + clock) into a clean 26-plane layout (last-move / check / repetition / clock / material / king position). Old models (e.g. checkpoint_498) keep working exactly as before through version-aware conversion, see §6.

1　state: a (7, 10, 9) int32 tensor

The environment's single board truth. Row 0 is at the top (Black's home rank), row 9 at the bottom (Red's home rank); values: −1 empty, 0–6 Red pieces, 7–13 Black pieces.

ChannelContentHow it is updated
state[0]Current board (the only "now")virtual_step: once a move lands, the history shift [1:6]=[0:5] runs first, then the move is executed — so layer [1] = the board before the previous move, and so on. The 6-layer history window is the information source of the last-move planes and the window-repetition planes; in the tree (search path) and in a game it is structurally identical by construction.
state[1..5]History boards of the last 5 moves
state[6]Ply count since the last capture (broadcast over 10×9)+1 on a non-capture, reset to zero on a capture. Draw threshold: see DRAW_NO_CAPTURE_PLIES = 120.

Terminology · ply: one move by either side counts as 1 ply; one full move = 2 plies; env.steps counts plies (code parameter names keep the term ply; Chinese prose writes 手). Note: state contains no side-to-move and no whole-game repetition counters — the side to move is an attribute of the environment/tree node; whole-game history lives in RuleContext (kept out of state, which is why opening-book npz files remain compatible across epochs).

2　Viewpoint normalisation (Red–Black symmetric encoding)

The network ever sees only one kind of "snapshot": the side to move is always at the bottom and always uses pieces 0–6. convert_to_network does it in three steps:

① Geometric flip: np.flip(state, axis=(1,2)) (180°, when current_player == 1)
Black's viewpoint = the board rotated half a turn; Black pieces come to the bottom
↓
② Piece renumbering: ID_SWAP_MAP (Black 7–13 ⇄ 0–6)
"side-to-move viewpoint" — one's own pieces are always 0–6
↓
③ Swap back after the network: switch_side_policy() = two action mirrors, left–right and up–down
the network's 2086-dim policy is flipped back to true coordinates before MCTS can consume it
Why swapping back is mandatory: the output-index semantics of the policy head are bound to the action encoding (global coordinates), while the snapshot frame has been flipped. Renumbering happens only in the "snapshot" layer — board state, action table and tree nodes always keep the global coordinates of the Red viewpoint. Every new feature plane must satisfy "still self-consistent in the flipped viewpoint" (all 26 planes do: broadcast planes are invariant by nature; single-cell planes flip with the board). This is precisely the precondition for the augmentation/mirroring machinery to hold globally.

3　Action encoding: 2086 = the geometrically reachable (from, to) pairs

Actions are a static, global, frozen ABI (the board representation may open a new feature epoch; the action encoding may not — four parties reference it: the Cython table, the JS engine, policy semantics and the mirror tables). Construction: init_class_dicts():

Key point: the encoding enumerates only square pairs that some piece type could geometrically reach, without regard to who moves. Chariot, cannon and pawn share the straight-line ranges — legality (whether one of your pieces actually stands on a square, horse blocks, cannon mounts, leaving one's own king en prise) is decided entirely at runtime by get_valid_actions. The action space therefore never changes with the rule set or the representation, and the 2086-dimensional semantics of the policy vector are stable across epochs.

4　Rule engine (epoch 2): the legal-move formulation

4.1 One adjudication settles three old problems

Epoch 1 used the capture-the-king convention: leaving one's own king en prise was legal, and the result was decided by capturing the enemy general on the next move; the perpetual-check loss was a hack matching ABAB patterns over the 6 history boards. Epoch 2 switches to the official rules:

legal moves = pseudo-legal moves − moves that leave one's own king attacked   # the R2 filter, done inside get_valid_actions

This single subtraction automatically delivers: flying general (facing generals = the king attacked along the file by the enemy general, no special case needed), pinned pieces cannot move, a unified expression of checkmate and stalemate (the side to move with an empty legal-move set loses — in Xiangqi, stalemated already loses), roughly 10~20% fewer wasted en-prise nodes in the search tree, and no more one-beat-late termination that has to "wait for the king to actually be captured".

4.2 game_over: the single entry point of termination adjudication

game_over(state, player_just_moved, action, ctx=None, valid_actions_next=None) → GameResult (relative to the side that just moved: 1 win / 0 draw / −1 loss / 2 not over), checked in priority order:

#ConditionVerdictScope
0A general is missing from the boardThe side without its general loses (defensive fallback impossible in normal play under R2)tree + game
1The side to move has no legal moveThe side that just moved wins (checkmate / stalemate unified)tree + game
2No-capture counter ≥ 120Drawtree + game
3Neither side has attacking pieces (chariot, horse, cannon, pawn)Drawtree + game
4The same position (board + side to move) appears for the 3rd time, and every occurrence has the side to move in check + the side to move is giving uninterrupted consecutive checks (≥ 3 plies; alternating check-and-quiet is not punished) + the move sequence repeats cyclically (the two gaps equal, and the move sequences of adjacent cycles identical move-for-move — equal spacing alone is just a reunion; a verbatim move repeat is a loop)the checking side (= the side that just moved) loses — perpetual checkgames only (needs ctx)
5The same position appears a 3rd time but it is not a cyclic perpetual check (incl. scattered repeats "all in check yet unevenly spaced")Draw — threefold repetitiongames only (needs ctx)
The split between in-tree and in-game adjudication is deliberate. A "repetition" reached by an MCTS leaf consists of hypothetical moves and is not a fact of the game, so in-tree nodes (ctx=None) apply only the position-level verdicts (1~3); threefold repetition and perpetual check are adjudicated at game level by env.step. The network perceives "a path recently repeated" through the window-repetition planes (19/20) of the 26 — a signal plane with an identical distribution in tree and in games; the punishment itself is not in the features.

4.3 RuleContext: the single source of truth for game-progression state

pos value format [count, chk_red, chk_black, t_prev2, t_prev1], shown against a real perpetual-check trace (the Black general at (0,4)/(0,3) dodging checks from the Red chariot; the tracked position is "Black general (0,4) + Red chariot (9,4) + Black to move"):

ply 1   first occurrence   [1, 0, 1, 1, 1]        both time slots filled with ply 1
ply 5   2nd occurrence     [2, 0, 2, 1, 5]        sliding window: prev-prev = 1, prev = 5
ply 9   3rd occurrence     verdict (first): total=2+1=3 ✓; checks=2+1=3==total ✓ (always in check);
                          9-5 == 5-1 ✓ (cyclic at equal spacing) → Red loses on perpetual check; then register (after): [3, 0, 3, 5, 9]

Why the third condition is necessary (found in a review conversation): the same position can well be revisited periodically along different paths — the Black general circles once to the left and once to the right while the Red chariot checks every move; every occurrence is in check and every gap is equal, yet the two circuits' move sequences differ, which fails the official standard of an unchanged cyclic move sequence. To that end the ledger also keeps a global move log move_log, and loop_ok() admits a repeated loop only when the last d plies are identical move-for-move to the d plies before them (seq[-d:] == seq[-2d:-d]); with insufficient data it passes leniently (only ever under-punishes, never mis-punishes). Regression cases test_equal_interval_different_moves_not_loop: left circuit + right circuit → draw; test_perpetual_check_lose: a true repeat → loss on ply 9; test_yijiangyixian_not_punished: alternating check and quiet → no punishment (all three cases re-verified for semantics via a game probe on 11-05).

Besides the ledger, RuleContext maintains run[Red/Black] — each side's current streak of consecutive checking plies (+1 on a check, reset by a move that does not check): a perpetual-check loss requires the side that just moved to reach run+1 ≥ 3. This is the engineering form of "uninterrupted consecutive checks": an alternating check–quiet pattern keeps run swinging between 0 and 1 and escapes punishment.

Rule sources (verified 2026-10-27): the definition of perpetual check — "consecutive uninterrupted checks forming a cycle that reaches three occurrences; a capture on the first move is not counted" (Wikipedia · 长打 (perpetual pursuit)); the general principles of the rules of repetition — "a unilateral perpetual check is never allowed", "where one move is forbidden and the other permitted, the forbidden side loses if it persists", "check then quiet is a permitted move pattern" (CXA Competition Rules of Xiangqi); on adjudication timing, "rule at the third occurrence of the same position" follows the engine-tournament convention (Pikafish Xiangqi Program Competition Rules; human tournaments "notify at three occurrences and demand a variation, loss on persistence" — self-play has no arbiter, so the equivalent direct ruling is used).

The always-zero chk_red is not waste: the key includes the side to move, so for a given key the checked side is always the same one; the two buckets keep separate books, which is what lets the test collapse to one line, checks == total.

MemberDescription
pos: dict[key, [count, chk_red, chk_black, t_prev2, t_prev1]]Key = current board bytes + side to move (same board, different side to move = two positions); value = occurrence count + "in check at occurrence" counts bucketed by side to move + ply indices of the last two occurrences (perpetual check requires the last three occurrences at equal spacing)

Why "consecutive cycles" need no extra windowCaptures strictly reduce material, and the key carries every board byte — no capture can occur between two occurrences of the same key (monotonicity of material), so the three occurrences necessarily fall inside one capture-free span of ≤ 120 plies; "accumulated scatteredly across the whole game" cannot happen by construction. Within the span, a loss is declared only after the "last three at equal spacing" test confirms a cyclic move sequence; scattered repetitions are scored as draws. Moreover, all occurrences of one key are delivered by the same opposing side ⇒ "in check at every occurrence" is automatically equivalent to "one and the same side checking over and over" — no move-level pattern matching needed
A mathematical guarantee on cycles: a cycle of period k plies must trigger threefold repetition within at most ~3k further plies; and even if both sides keep varying, the 120-ply clock provides the backstop. Every cycle's value settles within finite depth — precisely the precondition for a non-oscillating search. The remaining rules-of-repetition subtleties (perpetual chase, check-then-chase, etc.) are explicitly not implemented (a complexity cliff); all other repetitions are draws; self-play logs tally termination causes (checkmate / stalemate / draw-cause breakdown) — the rules are upgraded only if the data says so.

4.3.1 Why a "wandering check" (no repeated position) is not perpetual check

In the rules, the perpetual-check loss is at heart an adjudication over repeated positions: only the third occurrence of the same position (a position under review) gives the punishment something to attach to — "an unchanged cycle of moves, every one a check". If the position changes every move (the chariot switches routes, the general switches direction), the checked side faces a different escape each step; the game is still genuinely progressing, and punishing it has no basis in the rules — such lines slide naturally into the 120-ply clock draw, and the checking side gains nothing. What genuinely traps people in practice is the perpetual check that — because "the general has only 8 escape squares × the checking geometry is locked" — almost inevitably forms a cycle within a few moves; 3 occurrences + all in check + equal spacing is exactly the net that encloses it. Do not add a "consecutive check counter" variant: it would punish legitimate consecutive-check mates (every-move-checks attacking combinations are the soul of the game), the network is already fed perception signals by planes 18/19/20, and the clock is the natural cap on such lines.

4.4 The Python ⇄ Cython dual implementation

The hot path is all in C: get_valid_actions (pseudo-legal generation + make/test en-prise filtering on a temporary board) and is_check (direct-attack scan of the king square). king_attacked exists as mirror implementations on both sides (comments cross-anchored): any change on either side must be followed by the 2000-position random differential test in tests/test_chess_rules.py. Where C extensions are unavailable the code falls back to the Python version automatically (slower, identical semantics). The 44 legal moves of the initial position match the known Xiangqi value — a built-in cross-validation of the filter's correctness.

Data augmentation & mirroring

  • Training augmentation: augment_data left–right mirrors (plane tensor, policy, v) (the only symmetry operation in Xiangqi: a vertical flip would swap the sides); the policy is re-ordered through the 2086-dim permutation table. Augmentation happens on write into the buffer (×2).
  • Random mirroring during search: in self-play, random_mirror_state flips the board before each inference request and flips the policy back afterwards — the same position enters the network in both mirrors, equivalent to free augmentation (this is the source of the network's approximate LR-mirror equivariance acting as regularisation; a coherent equivariant loss is a possible future upgrade).
  • All 26 planes support general geometric transforms: broadcast planes are invariant by nature; single-cell and spatial planes flip with the board and keep their semantics.

6　feature_version: every model lives in its own feature epoch

Core discipline: planes and models come in pairs; never mix feeds. The 26 planes are not an "append-superset" of the 20 — channel 14 is a difference plane in epoch 1 and the last-move origin in epoch 2. The same channel number carries different semantics, so a channel-slicing compatibility scheme was rejected (it would feed wrong features silently, far more toxic than a crash), replaced by version-aware conversion.
Identity registration: NetConfig carries feature_version (new models = current epoch; v1 checkpoints without the field = 1). Double-written at the top level of the checkpoint. load_from_checkpoint restores it to model.feature_version → engine.feature_version
↓
Generate feature planes by epoch: convert_to_network(state, player, version) — version=1 runs the original 20-plane code path (channel-for-channel identical to v1, pinned by regression tests), version=2 runs the 26 planes; unknown epochs raise
↓
feature_version travels with the engine: the MCTS tree carries planes_version (passed at tree construction, propagated to nodes); AIServer / evaluation matches / self-play all set planes_version=engine.feature_version. The engine entry point validates the channel count and errors out outright on mismatch (crash rather than stay silent)

Opening: reset() (the initial position is registered as occurrence #1); or the opening book rewriting state + register_current_position()
↓
Search: NeuronMCTS(planes_version = the engine's feature_version); node init fetches valid_actions (R2); a leaf runs game_over(ctx=None) → if terminal, use z directly; otherwise convert_to_network → queued inference → (policy, value)
↓
Sampling: π = visit distribution (T=1), v = 0.5·z + 0.5·q (the endgame blend; epoch 3 plans a λ-discount instead) → convert_to_network (the same function) → buffer.add (LR augmentation ×2)
↓
Committing the move: env.step → virtual_step → game_over(ctx) (game level: threefold repetition / perpetual check take effect here) → register this occurrence → switch sides
↓
Termination: the samples' v are rewritten with (0.5z + 0.5q) → into the buffer; consumed by the next round's fit

10　Known limits and open items

  • JS rules engine synchronisation (apps/web/js/env-chess.js): two-players-on-one-screen and front-end draw adjudication still use the old convention; they must be synced to §4 and checked by the differential test in test/web_parity. The backend play pipeline is unaffected.
  • The family of pending adjudication details: forbidden repeating move patterns from the CXA rules — perpetual mate, perpetual chase, check-then-mate, check-then-chase — are not implemented (the Asian rules forbid only perpetual check and perpetual chase, and only perpetual check is implemented here). Losing on persistent forbidden moves vs our draw affects the experience only when the opponent is human; RuleContext's run / time-slot records leave room to extend, and future work is just a new branch in game_over.
  • Whole-game repetition inside the tree: leaves carry only the window signal planes; once the transposition table (planned E5) lands, this can be upgraded to exact path-level adjudication.
  • Server-side adjudication in human vs AI: the AIServer path holds no RuleContext (the browser is authoritative for termination) — this item closes automatically once the JS rules synchronisation is done.
  • Opening-book npz: snapshots store 6 history layers + the clock, with no version tag; should L2 semantics change again they must be re-exported (the tooling is missing).
  • Performance roadmap principle (project decision): the code stays primarily Python; Cython is kept only in existing hot paths where the impact is large (move generation / check detection — a measured 30× gap); C-ification of things like convert is not done when the gain falls short of two orders of magnitude — numpy tricks at the Python layer are preferred.
  • Action direction parameterisation (epoch-3 candidate): two 90+90 source/target heads replace the 2086-dim fully connected layer, saving ~5.7M parameters; the output is still gathered into 2086 dims for the outside world — the ABI does not move.
  • Memory note: the Xiangqi buffer (300k entries) holds roughly 2.8 GB of states + 2.5 GB of policies — a high resident footprint; on low-memory machines, lower buffer_size first.
